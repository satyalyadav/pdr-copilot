Why a dedicated copilot/What separates this from another chatbot

  • Each session keeps photos, action notes and results in order, and sends recent history with every assessment.
  • The model picks a method card. Code rejects invented methods and steps.
  • Small adjustments with reassessment aim to catch mistakes early.
  • Guided capture and measured shape can be added to the same loop.

Checks in code, independent of the model

  • Identical photos never count as improvement.
  • A reported coating concern stops corrections.
  • No pull is offered on a reported high spot.

First photo, assessed on its own

Reading A crease across a broad low below the taillight.

Next adjustment

  1. Find the creased low below the taillight. Align a flexible crease tab along it, clear of the seam and bumper edge.
  2. Glue the tab and set the lifter as the product instructs. Apply one small controlled lift, then release.
  3. Stop before the low turns into a raised ridge. Take a new photo before any further lift.
Tools
Glue-pull lifter, flexible crease tab, adhesive, release agent, PDR light.
Expect
The crease and surrounding low soften. One lift will not finish the repair.
Stop if
Paint changes, a ridge rises or the tab will not sit on the low. Never bridge the seam.

Later photo, reassessed

Change Looks flatter. Lighting and crop differ, so the change is tentative.

Next step

  1. End pulling. Sweep the PDR light over the former dent and its edges for reflection breaks.
  2. Run the required paint inspection. A smoother photo does not confirm a finished repair.

Test the guidance first, then real repairs

Define success firstFinish standard, damage limits and acceptable gap to the expert baseline.

1. Guidance review on recorded cases

  • Experts rate correctness, usefulness and safety.
  • Repeat the same inputs to check agreement.
  • Compare with a general chatbot to test added value.

Why first. Recorded cases test usefulness and reliability cheaply, before using panels and technician time, or risking damage.

Continue if the guidance passes review

2. Controlled scrap-panel pilot

One dent type; matched panels, material, tools, access and lighting.

  • Novices with the copilot
  • Novices alone
  • Experienced technicians, as baseline

Independent judges score each finish without knowing the group

Randomly assign novices to one group each.

Measure outcomes. Test the hard assumptions.

Success measures

Compare all groups against the expert baseline.

  • Quality. Repairs passing the finish standard.
  • Speed. Time to pass, including photos and AI.
  • Consistency. Variation across repairs and operators.
  • Damage. Rate of paint damage or new high spots.
  • Expert gap. Difference on each measure.
