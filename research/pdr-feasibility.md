# Paintless dent repair: perception and action feasibility

Research note for the November 5, 2026 interview. Scope: a fixed camera and reflection light, iterative human adjustment, and a useful demonstration without repair equipment. This is an experiment design, not evidence that novices can match expert quality, speed, or consistency. Those outcomes need a controlled study, including novices who remain dependent on assistance.

Follow-up research subsequently inspected sampled frames from four short repair clips and found a transcript with intermediate repair stages. See [repair sequences and the proposed technician trial](pdr-repair-sequences.md) for the updated source status. Download and inspection statements below describe the initial research pass.

## Recommendation

In two days, demonstrate **reflection comparison plus a human decision**, preferably using a permitted, fixed-view PDR sequence with a known unchanged light. Show detected stripe centerlines and local deviations from a baseline, with an uncertainty/invalid-capture state. Let an expert identify the next area to inspect; display their rationale and a source-linked instruction as a recorded expert annotation. After a recorded adjustment, compare again. Do not present a predicted push, force, high/low label, millimeter depth, or repair-quality score as measured. If a valid sequence is unavailable, use the synthetic fallback below and say so prominently.

This tests the narrow question: can a novice and expert use the same localized reflection evidence to agree on what to inspect next, while rejecting capture changes? It does not test whether the system can prescribe a safe repair or reduce repair time. Use a scripted clip or interactive frame selection, not a fabricated real-time live feed.

An expert annotation is optional for the interview prototype. If no expert is available, let the user mark an inspection region and have the LLM explain measured image observations using retrieved training passages. Label an illustrative repair step as hypothetical. Do not invent an expert endorsement. A small curated document collection is sufficient; model training and a large retrieval system are unnecessary for this experiment.

## Grounded guidance and vehicle constraints

The LLM should explain structured CV observations, cite the relevant passage, and ask for a better view when evidence is insufficient. It should not supply its own numerical depth, high/low classification, tool placement or force. [The Ding King's lighting lesson](https://onlinepdrtraining.thedingking.com/paintless-dent-repair/module-b-explanation-of-lights-and-reflections/) gives distinct setup instructions for a reflector board and an LED dent light. Its reflection-reading rules depend on the specified placement. This supports testing a known lighting setup rather than applying a universal bright/dark rule to uploaded photographs.

[Rivian's February 2, 2023 PDR statement](https://oemonestop.com/sites/default/files/Rivian_POS_Paintless-Dent-Repair%282-2-23%29.pdf) covers 2022+ R1T and R1S vehicles. It recommends non-damaging glue pulling to avoid damage from push rods, prohibits drilling, cutting or modifying panels for access, and specifies 30X paint inspections before and after repair. Guidance must resolve the applicable model, material matrix and current procedure before presenting a physical repair option. An ordinary uploaded photo cannot establish that the paint passes the required inspection. Generic training instructions do not override vehicle-specific constraints.

The [GYS training manual](https://www.gastec.is/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/GYS053168-MANUAL.pdf) was available through indexed excerpts, including section 4.3 on reflection interpretation, but direct PDF access returned 403. Do not treat the full manual or its illustrated procedures as inspected in this research.

## Round 2: perception to action

These outputs have different meanings:

1. A **bounding box or mask** identifies an image region, with no physical shape or direction.
2. A **reflection-distortion proxy** reports stripe displacement or curvature in pixels relative to a chosen reference. A panel's original body curvature and the light geometry also affect it. The proxy is not depth. Optical flow reports image displacement, which can arise from moving the camera or light and is not repair progress.
3. **Surface slope** requires calibrated pattern correspondence and geometric assumptions. A slope map is not a height map.
4. **Metric height** requires additional geometry and a reference or boundary condition; a signed height relative to the undamaged panel requires a credible target surface. Arbitrary photos cannot establish high versus low.
5. A **physical action** needs a tool, accessible side, location, direction, force and duration, plus paint condition, material and support constraints. Even a known height map does not uniquely identify those inputs.

[Inverse finite-element research](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17415977.2011.639454) estimates contact forces from measured displacement using a model and regularization; it is not a PDR action planner. [RECOLO](https://recolo.readthedocs.io/en/latest/overview.html) demonstrates a related measured-deformation-to-load problem only in its stated elastic regime. A [published patent application](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2025027539A1/en) describes target contours and repair assistance, but an application is a proposal, not evidence that a PDR policy works. Plasticity, hidden residual stress, prior deformation, bracing, tool access and unknown material make inverse repair harder than locating a distorted reflection. In the targeted search, no openly licensed dataset with synchronized fixed-camera PDR frames, individual tool actions, force and validated outcomes was verified; this is a search result, not a claim that none exists.

For real examples to discuss with an expert, [Dent Press](https://www.dentrepaircharlotte.com/before-and-after) publishes before/after clips, and [Jim the Dent Guy](https://www.jimthedentguy.com/how-pdr-works) describes repeated individual pushes while watching a reflection. These sources show the workflow, but fixed pose, synchronized individual actions, force measurements and reuse rights were not established. Seek permission and original footage before embedding. Videos and before/after photos alone do not establish an action label or quantitative improvement.

## Concrete demo and validation

- **Day 1:** Prepare three synthetic frames from a known virtual surface: initial, adjusted and unchanged surface with the light or camera deliberately shifted. Draw stripes with a simple documented image warp. Trace centerlines, fit a smooth reference outside a manually selected region, and show local pixel residuals. Expose the assumed reference and uncertainty; the reference fit may absorb real panel curvature or damage.
- **Day 2:** Add frame-to-frame comparison, provenance labels, source-backed explanations and a capture-validity gate. Include a recorded expert annotation only if one is available; otherwise label the user-selected region and illustrative action explicitly. If a genuinely fixed real sequence and permission arrive, add it as a separate qualitative example; never treat a public before/after pair as registered without evidence. Keep the known virtual height map separate from the pixel proxy so the demo can reveal where the proxy fails.
- **Capture gate:** Fix camera, panel and light; lock exposure/focus and retain an unchanged baseline. Track several non-reflective fiducials on the panel and independent fiducials on the light or its mount, outside the damage region. Reject comparison if panel/camera registration, light pose, occlusion, saturation, blur or stripe visibility fails a threshold chosen on repeated no-change captures. Panel markers alone cannot detect light motion. If light pose cannot be observed independently, report it as unverified and refuse a progress claim. Registration can correct limited camera motion only after validation; a homography cannot generally recover arbitrary 3D pose, reflections or light movement.
- **Acceptance experiment:** On synthetic fixtures, verify unchanged input is stable; intentional virtual surface change localizes where expected; identical geometry with moved camera or moved light is rejected; missing markers, glare and occlusion produce an invalid or uncertain result. Include a curved baseline, a change outside the region, and a case where the proxy worsens despite a virtual height reduction. Report false rejects and false accepts over the small test set. For a later real test, use repeated fixed captures, controlled perturbations and independent physical measurements plus blinded expert review. No physical accuracy, expert parity or time saving follows from the demo alone.

## Interview questions and search record

Ask the expert what they infer from a reflection, which details determine the next adjustment, how they detect a misleading light angle, and what would make them stop or request another view. Ask for a permitted fixed-view sequence with explicit action timestamps and whether an independent reference surface or measurement exists. What error, delay and false-reassurance rates would make the tool useful? What tasks should remain expert-only? A later study should compare novice-with-assistance, novice-alone and expert baselines on finish quality, repair time, rework and consistency, across dents and operators.

Round 1 searched the supplied records, deflectometry sample data, reconstruction and simulation repositories. Round 2 searched PDR repair videos and before/after examples, inverse mechanics, and planning publications. The sources above are the most relevant hits checked. No large archives, models, or datasets were downloaded, and no implementation was run. The DaRUS landing page and ScienceDirect page were inaccessible; licensing and actual capture properties remain open where stated.
