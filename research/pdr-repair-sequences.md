## Plain-language experiment and later technician trial

The immediate experiment asks whether the software can notice a changed reflection and explain what remains uncertain. Start with one image, supply a later image, and display the observed difference. A replay follows a repair that already happened. It cannot prove that the software's advice would have caused that repair. A synthetic fallback uses a chosen virtual dent and deliberately edited states; label those states computer-generated. A separate no-repair example with a changed camera or light tests whether the software gives false reassurance.

Calibrated deflectometry is a later measurement experiment. Display known stripes on a screen, view their reflection on a shiny test panel with a fixed camera, and measure how the pattern bends. First check the result against a known shape or an independent measurement. This asks whether the system measures shape accurately; it does not establish which tool action fixes it. See the [deflectometry review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5750795/).

For the later technician trial, use a fresh repair site on a separate comparable panel for each attempt. Record undamaged shape, create dents with the same controlled method, and measure initial shape to match cases by difficulty. Do not repair and re-dent the same site for the next participant. Allocate matched fresh panels across novice-with-AI, novice-without-AI and experienced-technician groups. Randomize novice assignment to assistance and panel allocation within matched groups; use multiple operators and repairs. The matching and randomization principle follows [NIST's guidance on randomized block experiments](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri332.htm). This particular PDR protocol is a proposed design, not a published trial result.

Have independent judges who do not know the group assess finish under standard lighting, alongside shape measurements against the original undamaged panel. Count total task time including AI use and setup, inspect new paint or panel damage, and compare variation across repairs. Set acceptable quality and time differences from expert performance before testing. A small pilot tests the protocol; an expert-parity claim needs an adequately sized study and uncertainty estimates that account for operator differences.

## Ranked candidates

1. **Dent Press, BMW rear quarter panel: publisher-described repair footage, later checked through sampled frames.** The [shop's gallery](https://www.dentrepaircharlotte.com/before-and-after) identifies a [before clip](https://www.dentrepaircharlotte.com/videos/bmw-dent-repair-rear-quarter-panel-indian-trail-nc-before.mp4) and a [roughly 30-second after clip](https://www.dentrepaircharlotte.com/videos/bmw-dent-repair-rear-quarter-panel-indian-trail-nc-after.mp4). Despite its label, the shop says the latter shows a rod working the panel. The sampled frames described above did not establish that action. Public download links are available, but no reuse license was established. The page says its cameras move along the panel; changing reflections and perspective could be mistaken for changes in dent shape. No force measurements or synchronized action labels are claimed.

2. **Dent Press, white SUV crease: paired endpoints, not a progressive sequence.** The [same gallery](https://www.dentrepaircharlotte.com/before-and-after) provides before and after stills and separate video clips for the same repair. It also identifies an R1T tailgate before/after pair. These are fallbacks for comparing assessments, not proof of intermediate states. The page's two R1S damage photographs are different views of damage, not a before/after repair sequence. Images and clips were not inspected for this entry. Reuse rights are unconfirmed.

3. **Automated dent repair patent: research lead, not a released experiment.** [CN116433572A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN116433572A/en) describes estimating a repair method and force, then scanning or photographing during repair and comparing with a reference model. This is a proposed system, not evidence that a real recorded sequence or force dataset is publicly available. No associated data or code was identified in this bounded search.

4. **Robotic incremental sheet forming: measured process data but not dent repair.** [Robotized Incremental Sheet Forming trajectory control](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mechatronics.2025.103360) describes experiments using a six-axis force/torque sensor and laser tracker to train a controller. It forms metal rather than repairing a dent. Public raw data, code and repair footage were not established. Likewise, the [LUH transfer press dataset](https://data.uni-hannover.de/en/dataset/press-set) offers force, displacement and resulting point clouds under CC BY 3.0, but records manufacturing of parts, not dent repair. Neither is evidence for PDR force or outcomes.
